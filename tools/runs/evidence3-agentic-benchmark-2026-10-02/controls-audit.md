# Evidence3 controls audit (read-only)

Date: 2026-10-02. Harness commit: `c3da7be` (full SHA `c3da7bec808f0e43c91ddbf90f7e4bfc3ab7665a`), read with `git show c3da7be:<path>` from the deployed checkout. The audit is read-only: it changed no harness, product or campaign file.

This is the full backing detail for the "Threats to validity" section of the main record (`README.md` in this directory): read this file for every citation and the per-control breakdown, and the main record for the summary a reader needs to bound the benchmark's claims. Structure and method mirror [Evidence2's controls audit](../evidence2-agentic-benchmark-2026-09-30/controls-audit.md).

## Summary

**The campaign ran the pre-registered design, under five amendments appended to the preregistration (A1 to A3 before any campaign session, A4 after a first attempt ended in a run-state failure, A5 after this attempt did too), and no control was set differently for the with-kmp-test and without-kmp-test arms of either agent.** Every claim below was checked against all 30 accepted session records, not a sample.

- **Complete, except two sessions.** 30 of 32 sessions were accepted and 2 are missing data: claude-code-8 was rejected by the harness's integrity checks (it never produced a result event, and a rejected cell is never replaced), and codex-cli-7 was lost to a failed guest call after 6 min 4 s (reason code `provider_runtime_real_worker_failed`) and left no evidence at all. Both are missing data under amendment A5: declared, not counted, not replaced. Of the accepted sessions none was terminated: the longest took 498 s against the 1,800 s provider timeout, and every exit code was 0. All 32 sessions ran one at a time and in the pre-registered order: the first-mover alternated between the two agents on all 16 pairs (Note H3). The frozen infra-flake classifier flagged 3 of the 30 cells it could classify, and its logic hash was recomputed from the deployed commit and equals the pre-registered value.
- **Same conditions in both arms.** Within each agent these were identical on every session, whichever arm it ran in: model, reasoning effort, CLI version, timeouts, spend cap, execution profile, permission mode, scenario prompt, product version, product commit, scenario commit, Gradle settings, seed, environment allowlist label, cache-state label and the guest VM. The arms differ only in the treatment: the kmp-test skill and its delivery, the prompt wrapper that asks for it, and the 5 `KMP_EVAL_*` variables the free arm drops by design (Note F7).
- **Agent state and transcript access.** Claude Code: the listing worked on 15 of 15 cells and no context-relevant file changed in any of them (0 entries under `projects/*/memory/` before any session). Codex CLI: the listing worked on 15 of 15 cells; the only changed context-relevant file was `config.toml` in 15 of them (amendment A3), so the harness's own flag `agent_state_clean` is false for those cells in the summaries and the record names them. The transcript access scan matched 0 of 30 cells (six patterns, section I).
- **Amendments.** A1 (the audit sidecar is validated with the scenario family, two scan patterns are added, and an accepted cell whose result subtype is not `success` counts toward the rejected-or-missing rule), A2 (the infra-flake stop rule becomes 6 or more cells instead of 3 or more) and A3 (a Codex CLI cell whose only changed context-relevant file is `config.toml` counts as clean) were appended before the first campaign attempt started; the canary preceded A2 and A3 and is not counted. A4 was appended after that attempt ended in a run-state failure and before this one, and records the incident and the decision to run the campaign once more, unchanged. A5 was appended after this attempt ended in a run-state failure of its own and before any result of it was read: it treats a session lost to a failed guest call as missing data, like a rejected cell, and lets a run-state failure caused only by such losses stand without voiding the campaign (no re-run, no make-up session). A2 loosens a stop rule that was set before the pre-run data was seen, and A5 changes how a lost session counts; the reasons and the data are in the amendments, and the sensitivity summary, which excludes flagged cells, is published next to the primary one (section I).
- **A first attempt failed; this is the second and last.** The first attempt (campaign `4e228608-ec59-44f5-9ddc-a40a33186ca4`, started 2026-10-01 at 17:41Z) ended with its LiveRunning state FAIL at 20:48:53Z: 19 sessions completed, one Codex CLI session ended in a transport failure (process timeout), and the last six rounds of both runtimes could not start because the host's elevated orchestration runner crashed (access violations in the .NET runtime of Windows PowerShell); the host also logged crashes of unrelated processes and corrected hardware errors, while another workload compiled native code on it. Under the preregistration nothing from that attempt is analyzed or published. This campaign (`2e96747d-ebb4-4642-b51f-9c1ec1bc1bba`) ran unchanged under a new id and is the only re-run.
- **This attempt's run state.** Its harness states BrokerReady to LiveAuthorized finished PASS. LiveRunning ended FAIL at 2026-10-02T00:59:15Z, and its only failed session is codex-cli-7, lost to a failed guest call after 6 min 4 s (reason code `provider_runtime_real_worker_failed`; the broker's response said the Hyper-V socket target process in the guest had terminated). Under amendment A5 that loss is missing data and the run-state FAIL does not void the campaign. The states EvidenceCopied and Closed did not run: the orchestrator's failure-safe closure copied every cell's record and audit (and the rejection) from the guest, then the operator copied the raw transcripts of the accepted cells through the broker, each with its size checked against its record, and ran the closure commands.
- **"Not recorded" does not mean missing from the run.** Those rows are either values fixed once in code or in the campaign manifest and applied to every session (flags, timeouts, retry policy, hooks, no turn cap), or things the vendor CLIs do not expose in this setup or do not let a harness pin (sampling settings, the model snapshot behind an alias, the login mode, provider-reported cost). None is set per arm. The two that can drift during a campaign, the served snapshot and sampling randomness, are spread across both arms by the interleaved pre-registered order. Host quiescence during the live window is an operational commitment backed by the operator's log, not a machine record (section I).
- **Claude vs Codex is descriptive only.** The agents differ by construction in tool surface, in how effort is set, in how turns, tool calls and tool output are counted, and in the token detail their usage events report for the cost estimate. Section 2 lists each difference so none is read as a capability finding.
- **The ground truth was on the guest, so the isolation control is detection, not prevention.** Section 1, E, and section 2 state exactly what the guest held and how the access scan covers it.

  - the classifier's logic hash was recomputed from the deployed commit and compared with the pre-registered value; the file hashes of the classifier and of the command classifier were recomputed with `git show` and compared too (all three equal the pre-registered values);

- **Arm labels,** as in the preregistration: A = product (`current-skill`, `product-assisted`), B = free (`no-skill`, `free-baseline-no-product`).

Per-cell records carry an absolute path of the guest's harness checkout (`resolved_kmp_test_executable_path`); that field is never published.

### Classification legend

- **controlled**: set by the harness or the design and verified identical (or identically and intentionally asymmetric, such as a treatment) across the cells it should hold across.
- **set-not-recorded**: set deliberately, but no per-cell field carries the value; recoverable only from the code (`[code]`) or the manifest (`[manifest]`).
- **uncontrolled-constant**: not set by the harness; fixed by a CLI, model or VM-image default, the same for every cell as far as this audit can tell.
- **uncontrolled-variable**: not set, and free to vary from cell to cell.

## 2. Asymmetries and caveats

These are true by design or by platform difference, not defects. They are listed so that no one is read as a capability finding.

1. **Equal effort label, not equal compute.** Both runtimes request reasoning effort `high`, through two different mechanisms (a harness-pinned CLI flag for Claude, `condition-launcher.mjs:128`; the model registry's default for Codex, `models/registry.json:50`), calibrated independently by two vendors. Equal labels do not imply equal effective reasoning compute, latency or quality.
2. **`num_turns` is not a cross-runtime metric.** Codex CLI reports one turn on every cell by construction of `exec` (`runtimes/codex-cli.mjs:415`); Claude Code counts real assistant turns. Report it per runtime only.
3. **Cost is a token-based list-price estimate for both runtimes.** `total_cost_usd` is `null` on every cell. The estimate uses the prices recorded in the preregistration (retrieved 2026-09-29 and re-checked on 2026-10-01). Codex CLI's uncached input is priced as a range, because OpenAI's pricing page says input tokens are either input, cached input or cache write and the usage event never says which; Claude's is a range between the five-minute and the one-hour cache-write rate.
4. **Tool calls and tool output are not the same quantity across runtimes.** Claude Code counts every `tool_use` (`Bash` and `Skill`; the Skill call the product wrapper forces is counted as a tool call and not as a shell command) and measures the tool results returned to the model. Codex CLI counts only `command_execution` items and measures the output of each command as logged; Codex may shorten what the model reads. The unit is labeled per runtime wherever the number is shown.
5. **The ground truth was on the guest; the control is detection.** The guest holds a full checkout of the deployed commit, including `corpus/expected/multi-module-test-failures.json` and the harness's test fixtures, and harness code on the guest reads the ground-truth file during a session. The agent runs as the same user with permission checks bypassed. A pre-session isolation probe exists in the repository (`checkIsolationProbe` in `isolation-probe.mjs`) but no code calls it, and this campaign claims no such probe. The transcript access scan runs after the campaign and found 0 matches in 30 cells; a clean scan shows that no transcript names the corpus, the preregistration or the private evidence, not that no session could have read them.
6. **The skill is older than the command.** The agents receive the skill snapshot of the `v0.15.0` commit, while `kmp-test` runs from the deployed `0.16.0` checkout. Both are fixed for the whole campaign.
7. **Known behaviors of the product on this scenario were found while establishing the ground truth and were not changed** (preregistration, section 2): `kmp-test` dispatches test tasks for 27 modules, not only the 11 the prompt asks for, and its own count of dispatched tasks is not the number of tests; the coverage tasks of three modules without unit tests fail with "no tests were run", which the envelope reports as `coverage_report_dispatch_failed`; and `kmp-test` 0.16.0 passes the string `undefined` as `ANDROID_SDK_ROOT` to Gradle when only `ANDROID_HOME` is set, which the harness avoids by setting both variables for every session.
8. **Amendment A2 loosened a stop rule after pre-run data.** The infra-flake rule was changed from 3 or more to 6 or more cells after the pre-run checks showed one guest Gradle daemon death in about 11 heavy runs. It was appended before any campaign session. The result for this campaign is 3 flagged cells: under the original threshold of 3 the campaign would have stopped and published nothing; under the amended threshold of 6 it did not. The amendment decided the outcome. The primary summary keeps every cell and the sensitivity summary excludes the flagged ones.
9. **Codex CLI state files (amendment A3).** Codex CLI rewrote `config.toml` at the start of every session (size unchanged, modification time changed); the harness starts Codex with `--ignore-user-config`, which, per Codex's documentation, skips that file, so a Codex cell whose only changed context-relevant file is `config.toml` counts as clean. Codex also updates `memories_1.sqlite` and other SQLite state files in every session. They are outside the listing's context-relevant set; Codex's documentation says local memories are off by default, the harness ignores the user configuration that could enable them and passes `--ephemeral`, and the harness does not open these files, so their contents are not verified.
10. **A first attempt failed before this campaign (amendment A4).** It consumed provider sessions (19 completed, one timed out) whose records stay private and unanalyzed; this campaign shares its deployed commit, design, scenario, prompts, timeouts and stop rules and ran under a new campaign id. To diagnose the failure, only the first attempt's run receipt (per-session statuses, exit codes, failure reasons, start and end times and process-output sizes) and the host's event logs were read; no key fact, answer, token count, cost or tool-call count of it was looked at before amendment A4. The operator did not clear the evaluation guest between the attempts, so unless the run's own preparation removed it (not verified), the first attempt's per-cell evidence (under its own campaign id, in the same private evidence directory) was on the guest's disk while this campaign ran; the harness has no access guard for it beyond the detection of the transcript scan, which includes the campaign's private-root path and matched 0 of 30 cells. A fault of the host (it logged corrected hardware errors and sporadic access violations during the first attempt) is a threat to any live session, and the stop rules detect its effects only after the fact.
11. **What the harness records is what it asked for, not what a provider honored.** It does not verify that Claude Code applies `--effort high`, that `--setting-sources ''` stops every file from being loaded, or that `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_AUTO_MEMORY=1` stops auto memory; the listing of `projects/*/memory/` is the only evidence on the last point, and it showed 0 entries.
12. **Inherited, not re-audited in depth.** Earlier audits raised items that this design does not target: Codex's `model_resolved` is an echo and not an observation; no dated model-snapshot pin exists for either runtime; the `cache_state` label is a literal; and the free arm's skill snapshot and shim exist as filesystem siblings of its workspace. They are carried over, not silently dropped.
13. **This attempt's run state and the rule that lets it stand (amendment A5).** Its harness states BrokerReady to LiveAuthorized finished PASS. LiveRunning ended FAIL at 2026-10-02T00:59:15Z, and its only failed session is codex-cli-7, lost to a failed guest call after 6 min 4 s (reason code `provider_runtime_real_worker_failed`; the broker's response said the Hyper-V socket target process in the guest had terminated). Under amendment A5 that loss is missing data and the run-state FAIL does not void the campaign. The states EvidenceCopied and Closed did not run: the orchestrator's failure-safe closure copied every cell's record and audit (and the rejection) from the guest, then the operator copied the raw transcripts of the accepted cells through the broker, each with its size checked against its record, and ran the closure commands. A5 was decided and appended before any key fact, answer, token count, cost or tool-call count of this attempt was read; it keeps every other stop rule, including "4 or more rejected or missing cells" (2 here), and it changes no metric. A5's own reason is that a run-state failure would otherwise void any long campaign on this host, where about one guest call in thirty failed across the two attempts while corrected hardware errors and crashes of unrelated processes were logged. A5 is a loosening of the preregistered rule on run states, made after the data existed but before it was read.
14. **Two sessions are missing, both in the free arm.** claude-code-8 was rejected by the harness's integrity checks (it never produced a result event, and a rejected cell is never replaced), and codex-cli-7 was lost to a failed guest call after 6 min 4 s (reason code `provider_runtime_real_worker_failed`) and left no evidence at all. Both are missing data under amendment A5: declared, not counted, not replaced. Under the design's order both fall in the free arm (rounds 8 and 7), so each agent's free arm has 7 counted sessions against 8 in its product arm; the group tables show the counts per group, and the infra-flake sensitivity summary can only lower them. No make-up session was run: it would add a temporal confound and cost hours for one cell. The cause of the lost session is not determined: the broker's response named a terminated Hyper-V socket target process in the guest, which a guest process crash or an action of the agent could both explain, and its evidence was not copied.
15. **An operator mistake during the closure.** The closure command that writes the cost estimate also prints it, and the operator's helper did not silence it, so the per-cell token counts of the 30 counted sessions (input, output, cache read and cache creation, by arm and cell index) were displayed to the operator before the stop rules were evaluated, against the rule that no result is read until they pass. No key fact, answer, duration, cost or tool-call count was displayed. The stop rules are mechanical and use none of it, A5 had already been appended, and the helper was corrected.
16. **The published summaries were regenerated after a bookkeeping fix.** The preregistration (section 8) has the analysis run from the deployed checkout, and it did: `campaign-summary.mjs` of the deployed commit grouped the lost session, which has neither a record nor a rejection and so no condition to take an arm from, in a group of its own with an unknown arm, which left Codex CLI's free arm declaring one session too few. The published `campaign-summary.json` and `campaign-summary-sensitivity.json` were regenerated with the repository's `campaign-summary.mjs` after a fix that assigns such a missing cell to its design arm (the arm the manifest's `round_order` gives its position), declared and not counted. The deployed tool's output is kept, and a field-by-field comparison shows that the only differences are that cell's arm and group (the group of unknown arm disappears, Codex CLI's free arm declares 8 and has one missing cell) and the place of its row in `cells[]`: the counted sessions (30 in the primary summary, 27 in the sensitivity one), every metric, every key fact and every provenance and limitation line are identical. This is a declared deviation from running the analysis from the deployed checkout, limited to that accounting; execution and grading of the sessions did not change.

## 3. Method

- Read every code file cited above at `c3da7be` with `git show c3da7be:<path>`, not from a working tree that might have moved on.
- Parsed all 30 cells' `record.json` and `audit.json` programmatically to compute every "identical across N cells" claim in section 1; none rests on fewer cells than stated.
- Cross-checked every `order_index` and condition pairing against the manifest's `round_order` and against the design tables in `scenario-campaign-plan.mjs`.
- Reconstructed the real dispatch order (Note H3) from the start times of all 32 sessions in the run receipt and checked it against `started_at` in the 30 records.
- Ran `infra-flake-classifier.mjs` and `transcript-access-scan.mjs` on the real closure directory (read-only; they write only to standard output) and recomputed the classifier's logic hash and the two file hashes from the deployed commit.
- Computed the agent-state facts from the `agent_state` object of every record; the listing is content-free (path, size, modification time), so this audit cannot say what a changed file contained.
- Did not open or search any cell's raw transcript beyond what the classifier and the scan read programmatically: this is a controls and provenance audit, not a transcript review.
- Did not re-run `isolation-probe.mjs` or `cost-estimate.mjs`; where this document cites their behavior it is from reading their source and from their closure output. `campaign-summary.mjs` ran twice, from the deployed checkout and, for the published summaries, from the repository after the bookkeeping fix of caveat 16.
